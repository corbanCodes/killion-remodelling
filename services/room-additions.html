Room Additions & Bigger Projects

The big ones need a plan, a permit and a real schedule. Rick will tell you what it takes before you commit to anything.

Handyman & Repairs Painting Decks & Porches Windows & Doors Tile & Carpet Trim & Carpentry Room Additions

Additions and finished basements are a different kind of job from a day of repairs, and they deserve a different kind of conversation. There is a drawing, a permit, an inspection schedule, trades to bring in at the right moment and a realistic start date. Anyone who gives you a firm number for this on the phone without seeing the house is guessing.

What a bigger project looks like

It starts with a walkthrough and a conversation about what you actually want the space to do. Then a written quote that itemises materials separately from labour, so you can see where the money goes and change your mind about a finish without the whole number moving. Then a start date he intends to keep, and a point of contact who is also the person swinging the hammer.

Where he brings in help

Rick does the carpentry, the trim, the flooring and the painting himself. Structural engineering, significant electrical and plumbing, HVAC and anything that needs a licensed trade and a stamped inspection get the right licensed person, scheduled in at the right point so they are not tripping over each other. You will know who is coming and when.

Questions

Room Additions — The Usual Questions

How accurate is the first number you give me?

The first number over the phone is a range, and he will call it a range. The written quote after a walkthrough is the one to plan around. If something unexpected turns up once a wall is open, you hear about it that day with a price attached, before the work goes ahead.

Who deals with permits and inspections?

Rick handles pulling permits and scheduling inspections for the work he is doing. Requirements differ between Bloomington, Normal and the smaller townships, and he will tell you what your particular job needs early rather than on the day.

Can I live in the house while you work?

Nearly always, yes. The space being worked on gets sealed off and the dust kept out of the rest of the house as far as is realistic. He will be honest about the days that are genuinely disruptive — the demo, the concrete, the floor sanding — so you can plan around them.

Do you do full kitchen and bathroom remodels?

No, and he would rather say so plainly than take the job and learn on your house. Full kitchens and bathrooms are their own specialism. He will happily do the parts that are his — the tile, the flooring, the trim, the painting, the door — alongside whoever is handling the cabinets and the plumbing.
